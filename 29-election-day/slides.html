Project: Gerrymandering

Project: Gerrymandering

Research question:

Will the adoption of the new district map lead to an increase in partisan advantage relative to 2024? If so, by how much?

You answer will take the form of a dashboard.

Prop 50 Dashboard

Your final deliverable is a dashboard that:

  1. Makes it easy to compare the 2024 congressional results with a hypothetical re-run of the election using the new district map.
  2. Presents two maps, one using the 2024 districts and the other using the new districts.
  3. For both the observed 2024 election results and the hypothetical re-run, presents at least three summary statistics:
    1. Number or proportion of seats won by Democrats (or Republicans)
    2. Mean-median score
    3. Efficiency Gap
  4. Includes a “Methodology” section that describes the data source, the data cleaning procedure, the estimation process, and caveats about inferring gerrymandering from single statistics.

A tale of two precinct types

SV Precincts

  1. Created by counties each election
  2. Two counties might use the same precinct name
  3. Unique ID: SVPREC x FIPS
  4. May not correspond to geographic area Example:
    • SVPREC: 30010 holds the vote by mail
    • SVPREC: 30010A holds the in-person votes

    This is why we won’t have shapefiles for every precinct

A tale of two precinct types

SR Precincts

  1. Created by the statewide data base to have a stable precinct unit from election to election.
  2. One SR precinct is generally an aggregation of smaller SV precincts.

We can get both votes and shapefiles for SR precincts.
